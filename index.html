How it works

  1. 1
    Upload

    Choose a stained blood smear image with one white blood cell near the centre.

  2. 2
    Compare

    A neural network in your browser compares it with patterns learned from thousands of cell images labelled by clinical pathologists [1].

  3. 3
    Read

    You get a score for each cell type. On 960 held-out images from the same dataset, the top score matched the pathologists' label 97.5% of the time (see results). Expect lower accuracy on images from other labs or stains.

Private by design

Your image is analyzed in your browser and is never uploaded or stored. The page loads its code and fonts from public CDNs (jsDelivr and Google Fonts). Hemra has no accounts, cookies or analytics.

Research only

Hemra is an educational tool, not a medical device. It learned only from normal cells of people without infection, blood disease or cancer [1], so it cannot detect leukemia or any other condition. Its scores are not the chance that anyone is ill.
